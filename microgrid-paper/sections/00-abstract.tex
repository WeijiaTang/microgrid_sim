\begin{abstract}
Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) has emerged as a promising approach for microgrid battery dispatch, yet existing validation paradigms assess policies solely through cumulative economic cost---a metric insufficient when trajectory morphology, safety compliance, and intervention diagnostics critically determine operational viability. We present a morphology-aware validation framework integrating Shield-Aware Intervention Learning with Soft Actor-Critic (SAIL-SAC) and a multi-gate protocol (P4 BalancedGate) that evaluates policies across mid-band state-of-charge dwelling, price-responsive cycling, infeasible-action suppression, and logged intervention metrics. Tested on an IEEE 33-bus distribution network with cross-year generalization (2023 training, 2024 evaluation) across 16 random seeds, six successful policies (37.5\% success rate) achieve mean objective cost $11.788 \pm 0.120$\,M\textyen{} with $20.4 \pm 15.6$\% mid-band dwelling and $6.1 \pm 7.3$\% infeasible actions under the simple efficiency-based model. Cross-fidelity testing shows small objective variation across battery representations, with mean cost changing from 11.788\,M\textyen{} in the simple model to 11.737\,M\textyen{} in the full Thevenin model. The logged P4 evaluation records zero deployed shield activation but a nonzero inventory-teacher action gap ($0.301 \pm 0.087$), showing why intervention diagnostics must be reported separately from cost. The framework establishes morphology-aware validation as a necessary complement to cost-based selection, enabling battery dispatch policies that balance economic performance with interpretable, safety-compliant behavior.
\end{abstract}
