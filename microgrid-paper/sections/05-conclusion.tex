\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This study addresses an evaluation gap in deep reinforcement learning for battery storage control: cumulative economic cost alone cannot characterize operational behavior when safety layers, action feasibility, and battery morphology affect evaluation outcomes. We presented a morphology-aware validation framework integrating SAIL-SAC (Shield-Aware Intervention Learning with Soft Actor-Critic), Inventory Teacher diagnostics, and P4 BalancedGate multi-dimensional checkpoint selection. The framework was evaluated on an IEEE 33-bus distribution network, with main results reported for six P4-selected candidates that satisfy the pre-declared morphology gates.

\subsection{Key findings}

\textbf{Morphology-aware selection defines evaluation candidates.} The six reported P4 seeds satisfy the pre-declared morphology gates (mid-band dwelling $>5\%$, infeasible actions $<30\%$) and form the reported basis for the case study. In DRL reliability assessment, reporting trajectory morphology and intervention diagnostics, rather than scalar reward alone, can better distinguish low-cost checkpoints with limited trajectory quality.

\textbf{Cost and morphology.} The six P4-selected seeds recorded mean objective cost $11.788 \pm 0.120$\,M\textyen{} under the simple EBM. Mean objective costs under higher-fidelity battery settlement remained close to the simple-model value: 11.740\,M\textyen{} for Thevenin loss-only, 11.739\,M\textyen{} for Thevenin Rint-only, and 11.737\,M\textyen{} for Thevenin full PBM. The simple-to-full mean difference was $-0.43\%$, an accounting effect of replaying the same checkpoint under different achieved-power, SOC, and terminal-penalty settlement equations rather than evidence of lower physical operating cost. However, mid-band SOC dwelling varied 8.5--49.1\% across P4-selected seeds with comparable costs, showing that economically similar policies can exhibit qualitatively different operational trajectories.

\textbf{Intervention diagnostics clarify interpretation.} The full-year P4 evaluation records zero shield activation in the six P4-selected seeds, while the normalized inventory-teacher action gap remains $0.301 \pm 0.087$ and infeasible-action dwell remains $6.1 \pm 7.3$\%. The data therefore do not support residual shield activation, but they do show incomplete alignment with the teacher reference and nonzero raw-action infeasibility. Reporting zero shield activation is still part of the evaluation, because it prevents shielded execution, teacher guidance, and policy autonomy from being conflated.

\textbf{Price-responsive behavior is heterogeneous.} Mean peak-price discharge fraction is 24.7\% and mean valley-price charge fraction is 27.7\% under the simple EBM, with large inter-seed variability. Seed 42 shows high peak discharge, but several other P4-selected seeds meet the morphology gates with lower price-directional behavior. The results therefore point to improved SOC morphology and feasibility suppression in the selected candidates, while also showing that intertemporal arbitrage learning remains incomplete.

\subsection{Methodological implications}

The P4 BalancedGate protocol selects policies by balancing economic performance with interpretable operational behavior. Morphology-aware selection cannot \emph{create} well-performing policies from limited candidates---it can only \emph{select} and \emph{rank} candidates from a training pool. For evaluation-oriented reporting, multi-seed candidate training and explicit morphology assessment are therefore more informative than reliance on single-run results.

Explicit intervention diagnostics change the evaluation narrative. Traditional safe RL framing often treats shields as invisible infrastructure and reports only final evaluated performance. This study treats shield activation, teacher-action gap, and infeasible-action dwell as operational metrics rather than implementation details. For battery storage, where SOC trajectory is itself an observable operational quantity, this reporting helps indicate where autonomous learning ends and protective or heuristic intervention begins.

The validation protocol is also separable from the SAC backbone. PPO, TD3, Lagrangian safe RL, or CBF-based controllers could be passed through the same held-out morphology audit if they expose raw actions, executed actions, feasibility flags, and intervention or constraint-residual diagnostics. For methods without an explicit action shield, the corresponding audit variable would be the Lagrangian constraint residual, barrier-function correction, or safety-filter adjustment rather than $\Delta a_{\mathrm{sh}}$.

\subsection{Limitations and future work}

\textbf{Seed ensemble size.} The available multi-seed candidate scope remains insufficient for formal statistical inference on protocol ablations. Modern DRL evaluation standards recommend 20--30 seeds for reliable comparisons\cite{agarwal2021statistical}; computational cost constraints limited this study to an engineering evaluation centered on the six P4-selected candidates. The main tables are conditional on passing the P4 morphology gates and should be read as an assessment of selected candidates rather than as an unconditional training-reliability estimate for SAC.

\textbf{Testbed scope.} The IEEE 33-bus network represents a single feeder topology under one network-stress profile with fixed BESS and PV placements. Generalization to other topologies (radial vs.\ meshed, urban vs.\ rural load profiles), storage-to-network ratios, and operational regimes (voltage support, frequency regulation, resilience mode) remains outside the present validation scope. The present network audit records power-flow failures but does not fully characterize annual voltage-margin, line-loading-margin, feeder-loss, or sensitivity distributions. The cross-fidelity testing (simple $\to$ Thevenin full) is consistent with limited model sensitivity within the IEEE 33 context, but it does not establish robustness to topological diversity, temperature-dependent electrochemical parameters, aging, or capacity fade.

\textbf{Oracle assumptions and online uncertainty.} The LP oracle baseline assumes perfect forecast and operates under relaxed battery dynamics. It serves as an optimistic reference for diagnostic indexing, but it does not reflect practical operational constraints such as forecast error, real-time market participation, or multi-hour commitment. Operation under load, PV, and price forecast uncertainty remains for separate evaluation and may reduce both economic recovery and morphological quality. Future deployment studies should also test initial-SOC perturbations, abnormal weather days, rolling-horizon execution, and changes in available BESS power or energy capacity.

\textbf{Policy internalization.} The current framework exposes incomplete policy internalization through nonzero teacher-action gap, nonzero infeasible-action dwell, and limited average price response. Future work can investigate methods for reducing these gaps: late-stage training with reduced teacher guidance, explicit infeasible-action penalties that better align with evaluation metrics, or meta-learning over morphology penalty schedules. A second extension is to replace the directional peak/valley fractions with power-weighted price-response scores that account for charge--discharge depth during extreme-price periods. The goal is not eliminating safety layers, which remain appropriate for hard constraints, but shifting more responsibility to the learned policy for soft preferences such as mid-band dwelling and price-responsive timing.

\subsection{Implications for DRL battery-control evaluation}

For DRL battery-control evaluation, multi-seed candidate training is preferable from the outset because single-seed or three-seed studies are sensitive to selection bias. The morphology gates used to select operational candidates are more informative when reported together with intervention diagnostics, including shield activation, teacher-action gap, and infeasible-action dwell. These quantities can be treated as operational outcomes alongside economic cost, rather than as implementation details. Cumulative reward can therefore be supplemented by trajectory-level diagnostics such as mid-band dwelling, boundary avoidance, price-responsive directionality, infeasible-action suppression, and power-flow failures. For policies trained on simplified battery models, higher-fidelity battery settlement helps bound broader operational claims.

Within the tested IEEE 33-bus setting, morphology-aware validation provides an additional assessment layer for DRL battery policies that balance economic performance with interpretable, constraint-aware operational behavior. By making battery morphology and intervention diagnostics explicit, the framework helps determine whether a learned policy warrants further evaluation, additional training, or replacement by another candidate from the training pool.
