\section{Case study and results}
\label{sec:case_study}

The case study evaluates SAIL-SAC with P4 BalancedGate validation on the modified IEEE 33-bus feeder under network-stress conditions. Training uses 2023 load, PV, and price data over 100\,k steps, with validation checkpoints every 2500 steps across four seasonal 30-day windows. Final evaluation uses the full 2024 year (365 days) to assess cross-year generalization. The reported results focus on the six seeds that satisfy the pre-declared P4 morphology gates; these candidates define the assessment set used in the main tables and figures.

\subsection{Six-seed evaluation and selection characterization}

Table~\ref{tab:p4_selected_seeds} reports the six P4-selected candidates used throughout the main evaluation. These seeds satisfy the pre-declared morphology gates: mid-band SOC dwelling $>5\%$ and infeasible-action fraction $<30\%$. The reported evaluation is therefore conditional on passing the morphology-aware validation protocol, rather than a characterization of every random initialization.

The six selected seeds display morphological diversity. Seed 93 records the largest mid-band dwelling (49.1\%, with 0\% infeasible actions), while seeds 81, 83, and 84 exhibit more conservative mid-band residence (8.5--10.7\%) with differing infeasible-action fractions. Seed 42 represents an additional selected profile with 26.7\% mid-band dwelling and the largest peak-price discharge response among the six seeds. Together, these profiles show that selected policies can adopt different operational patterns while maintaining comparable economic performance, as also seen in Figure~\ref{fig:morphology_scatter}.

\subsection{Economic performance and cross-fidelity behavior}

Table~\ref{tab:six_seed_performance} reports the consolidated performance of the six P4-selected seeds across four battery model fidelities. Mean objective cost is $11.788 \pm 0.120$\,M\textyen{} for the simple EBM baseline, where M\textyen{} denotes million CNY-equivalent objective units. The corresponding higher-fidelity values in this logged dataset are 11.740\,M\textyen{} (Thevenin loss-only), 11.739\,M\textyen{} (Thevenin Rint-only), and 11.737\,M\textyen{} (Thevenin full PBM). The lower higher-fidelity mean reflects an engineering settlement effect, not a standalone physical advantage of the higher-fidelity model. The same selected checkpoint is replayed under different battery-settlement equations, so loss-aware and resistance-aware dynamics change the achieved terminal power written into the feeder, the SOC integration path, and the terminal-inventory penalty before the objective is accumulated. The simple-to-full mean difference is $-0.43\%$, consistent with comparable full-year performance under higher-fidelity battery settlement for the reported P4-selected candidates.

\subsection{Intervention diagnostics and price-response behavior}

Table~\ref{tab:shield_dependence} reports intervention-related diagnostics for the six P4-selected seeds under the simple EBM full-year evaluation. In this P4 evaluation, \texttt{shield\_enabled=0} and all logged shield-activation fields are zero. The logged evidence therefore indicates no residual shield activation in this result set. The only nonzero intervention diagnostic reported here is the inventory-teacher action gap, $g_{\mathrm{IT}}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:inventory_teacher_gap}. The six P4-selected seeds have mean absolute normalized gap $0.301 \pm 0.087$, showing that the learned actions remain distinct from the rule/teacher reference even when the corrective shield is not active.

The six-seed evaluation also qualifies the price-response interpretation. Mean peak-price discharge fraction is 24.7\%, and mean valley-price charge fraction is 27.7\%, with large inter-seed dispersion. Seed 42 records high peak discharge (75.1\%), but several other selected seeds satisfy the morphology gates with lower price-directional behavior. Overall, the P4-selected set retains policies with gate-satisfying SOC morphology and infeasible-action suppression, while price-responsive arbitrage remains uneven across seeds.

\subsection{Baseline comparison}
\label{sec:external_baselines}

Table~\ref{tab:external_baseline_comparison} compares P4 BalancedGate against two within-family baselines: SAC with the same shield but cost-only checkpoint selection, and vanilla SAC without corrective shielding. The baseline columns are diagnostic rather than paired statistical comparisons because the seed sets and shield settings differ. The comparison is intentionally limited to a mechanism check, not a comprehensive algorithm leaderboard. The perfect-foresight LP schedule remains a normalization anchor for recovery indexing rather than a claimed deployable comparator. The cost-only SAC+shield baseline treats the shield as invisible infrastructure and selects by scalar reward. The vanilla SAC baseline removes corrective shielding, retaining only physical battery clipping.

\subsection{Operational interpretation}

The case-study scope is deliberately limited, and those limitations are visible in the reported diagnostics. The six P4-selected seeds record low objective cost ($11.788$\,M\textyen{} mean simple-model cost), zero power-flow failures, 20.4\% mean mid-band dwell, and 6.1\% mean infeasible-action dwell. Their morphological range---8.5\% to 49.1\% mid-band dwelling---shows that the P4 protocol separates profiles with different operational behavior. At the same time, the full-year evaluation shows zero shield activation and a nonzero inventory-teacher gap, which is more consistent with incomplete internalization relative to the teacher reference than with residual shield activation. The LP oracle normalization reference assumes perfect foresight; the resulting recovery index is a diagnostic rather than an optimality certificate.

Figure~\ref{fig:morphology_scatter} and Figure~\ref{fig:cross_fidelity_heatmap} summarize the two main result views: the former shows both the gate-plane positions and per-seed morphology profiles for the six reported candidates, and the latter shows that cross-fidelity variation remains small across those candidates, indicating limited cross-fidelity sensitivity without implying monotonic degradation.
